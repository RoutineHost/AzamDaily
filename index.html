Which body parts does your weekly plan cover?

Your plan trains the major muscle groups across the week. Strength sessions cover upper-body pushing and pulling, legs and core; lighter days support cardio, mobility and recovery.

About the ratings: Scores are practical usefulness ratings out of 5 for your goals and home equipment—not popularity statistics or a measured percentage of people who do each exercise. They reflect muscle coverage, usefulness, ease of progression and suitability for a beginner. Safe technique and consistency matter more than the score.

How to use this planner

Choose any day from the seven buttons directly above. It jumps straight to that day's checklist. Do the warm-up, complete the listed exercises in order, then follow the food reminders below the workout. GIF demonstrations are embedded in this file.

Why not hard training every day?

Daily movement is good. Muscles also need recovery. This plan uses four strength sessions, two light days and one recovery day. If you feel unusually sore or tired, make the next day easier or rest.

How to use the timings
  • Warm up for 3–5 minutes before strength workouts.
  • Rest 60–90 seconds between strength sets; take up to 2 minutes if you need it to keep good form.
  • Exercise times are estimates, not a race. Move slowly and stop if you feel pain, dizziness or unusual breathlessness.
  • The daily total includes warm-up, rests and changing exercises. If you are short on time, do the listed exercises in order and don't rush the reps.

Goal: build a strong, fit body and support cardiovascular and metabolic health. No routine can guarantee that you will never develop a chronic disease, but these habits can lower several preventable risk factors.

  • Move regularly: gradually build towards 150 minutes of moderate cardio per week, such as brisk walking. Break up long sitting periods with 2–3 minutes of movement every 30–60 minutes where practical.
  • Strength train: follow the four planned strength days, with lighter movement and recovery on the other days. Use good technique and allow recovery.
  • Sleep: aim for a consistent 7–9 hours, including after night shifts.
  • Food pattern: favour vegetables, whole fruit, dal/beans, whole grains, nuts, eggs, fish, chicken or paneer. Keep salt, deep-fried foods, processed meats and highly processed snacks moderate.
  • Added sugar: choose unsweetened sattu and drinks; avoid routinely adding sugar or honey. You do not need to avoid whole fruit or plain milk.
  • Hydration: drink to thirst and increase sensibly with heat and sweating; don't force excessive water.
  • Health checks: keep routine checks with a clinician, including blood pressure and appropriate screening. Exercise does not replace medical care.

For a leaner-looking face and neck

There is no reliable face-fat or neck-fat spot-reduction exercise. If you have excess overall body fat, gradual overall fat loss may also reduce facial and neck fullness. Facial shape is also influenced by genetics, bone structure and normal fluid shifts. Avoid crash diets, dehydration, slimming products and aggressive jaw/neck routines.

If facial or neck swelling is sudden, persistent, painful, one-sided, or comes with trouble breathing or swallowing, seek medical advice promptly.

  • Your 3 spoons of sattu are fine. Honey is still added sugar; a teaspoon is a modest amount.

  • Weekly chips can fit in moderation. No need for all-or-nothing food rules.
About belly, face and neck fat

No exercise or food selectively removes fat from the belly, cheeks or neck. Core exercises strengthen abdominal muscles; overall body-fat change, genetics, sleep and daily activity influence how lean your face and waist look. Chin tucks help posture, not fat loss. Avoid aggressive jaw or weighted neck exercises.

Progress safely

Start with the lower end of each rep range. When all sets feel controlled and you can reach the upper end in two sessions, make the movement slightly harder or add resistance. Your 5 kg dumbbells may eventually be too light for some exercises. Do not swing or kip on your wall-mounted bar; have the wall, anchors and installation checked, and do not use it if it moves or loosens. Use assisted pull-ups until you can do clean reps.

Desk-work habit

During your shift, stand and move for 2–3 minutes every 30–60 minutes where practical. These short breaks help reduce long uninterrupted sitting, but still aim to build towards 150 minutes of moderate cardio each week. Keep your 7–8 hours of sleep after night shift.
